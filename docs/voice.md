# Voice

**English** · [简体中文](voice.zh-CN.md)

**Talking.** Press **⌘⇧S** (Ctrl+Shift+S on Linux and Windows; rebindable, and in the palette as **Talk**) or the microphone button, and say what you want. The microphone stays on until you press either again: each pause ends what you said, which is worked out in the background while it listens on for the next. With [Smart Turn](https://huggingface.co/pipecat-ai/smart-turn-v3) downloaded in **Settings → Voice** (9 MB), a pause is judged by how you said the last words: a finished sentence is acted on within half a second, and a pause to think, mid-sentence, is waited through for up to three seconds; it runs on this computer too, in `clew-speak`. What you said is then either one of the window's commands — said the way you would say it, in any order, with or without "open" or "show"; a command that costs something (an Explain All pass, starting or stopping the program, opening another folder) asks before it runs, and you can answer it aloud, "yes" or "no" — or a question, which goes to Ask and is asked at once (both can be turned off in **Settings → Voice**). Voice shows what it is doing in Ask's corner: the pill says it is listening, and after a command what it did, without opening the card; a question opens the card with its answer.

**Anything the window does.** Every command in the palette runs when you say its name, in the language the window shows, and so does each step of the debugger ("step over", "continue"), without a yes for each step. What needs a value is said with it, in English or the window's language:

| Say | What happens |
|---|---|
| "go to line 30", "line thirty" | The caret goes to line 30 of the open file |
| "scroll down", "scroll up 10 lines", "go to the end of the file" | The file moves by a screen, by lines, or to either end |
| "fold all", "unfold all", "fold here" | Folds close and open, as **zM**, **zR** and **za** do |
| "breakpoint on line 12" | A breakpoint is set (or cleared) on line 12 |
| "watch total", "stop watching total" | An expression is added to the debugger's watches, or removed |
| "find quantity", "find next", "close find" | The find bar looks for the words in the open file |
| "search for quantity" | The Search view looks through the whole project |
| "open cart dot py" | The project's file of that name opens |
| "go to apply discount" | The symbol of that name opens, however it is spelled (`apply_discount`, `applyDiscount`) |
| "voice settings", "full screen" | Settings opens at that page; the window changes |
| "change the app's language to French", "dark mode", "use one dark", "never read answers aloud", "use OpenAI" | The setting changes, as its control in Settings changes it — said in any of the window's languages ("把语言改成英文", "言語をフランス語に変更して") |
| "download", "not now", "trust and connect", "yes", "no" | The dialog on top answers: its buttons by their names, its question by a yes or a no |
| "open the third result", "the second one", "第三个" | The row of the list in view is picked by its place in it |
| "close", "cancel", "never mind" | What is on top goes away, as with **Esc**: a dialog, a menu, Time Travel, the find bar, Ask |
| "search by meaning for where the total is worked out", "filter files by cart", "walk me through checkout" | The views that take words are filled and run |

**Hands-free.** Whatever the window shows can be pressed by saying it: a dialog's buttons, a setting's switch or choice, Ask's answers to the agent's questions, a list's rows — by the words on them, or by the name of what they belong to when several rows have the same button ("remove rust-analyzer"). A dialog on top takes what you say as it takes the pointer, and a note or a condition being written takes your words as its text, until you say its **Save**. What removes or forgets something waits for a yes first, as a word may have been misheard; a question that asks to be read is not answered before it can be. These are yours alone: the agent in Ask reaches the palette's commands and the settings, never a question's Allow.

**On your machine.** The microphone is read through the system's own audio (Core Audio, WASAPI, ALSA — which PipeWire and PulseAudio serve), the audio is resampled to 16 kHz, cut to where there is speech, and transcribed by whisper.cpp on this computer, on the GPU through Metal on a Mac and on the CPU elsewhere. Nothing is sent anywhere; only a question you choose to ask goes to your language model, as typed ones do.

**Calling it by name.** Turn on **Start listening when you say “Hey, Clew”** in **Settings → Voice**, and download the **Keyword Spotter** it needs there (33 MB: k2-fsa's streaming Zipformer for English and Mandarin, Apache-2.0, checked against its published size and SHA-256). Then, whenever the microphone is not listening, it waits — its button drawn in the accent colour: say "Hey, Clew" and it listens, as if you had pressed the voice key, and "Hey, Clew, open the call graph" said in one breath works too. Say "Bye, Clew" or "Stop listening" and it waits again — as it does after three minutes with nothing said, when it was called by name; the voice key and the microphone button still turn listening on and off. One window waits at a time: the one in front. While it waits, what the microphone hears goes only to the keyword spotter, which runs in `clew-speak` on this computer and knows that one phrase: nothing is kept or turned into words.

**Models.** Whisper.cpp's own models, the same ones [Echo](https://github.com/damien-schneider/Echo) uses, downloaded only when you click a model's **Download** (its size is on the button) and accepted only if they are, byte for byte, the published file: **Small** (`small-q5_1`, 190 MB, the default), **Large Turbo** (`large-v3-turbo-q5_0`, 574 MB) and **Large** (`large-v3-q5_0`, 1081 MB), checked by their SHA-256 and exact size as Echo checks them, all of them multilingual; and Tiny and Base (English) for quick commands on slower machines, checked by the SHA-1 whisper.cpp lists. They are kept in Clew's data folder (`voice/`), and can be removed there. On macOS, the first time Clew listens, the system asks whether it may use the microphone.

**Answers, aloud.** The answer to a question you asked by voice — or, if you choose, to every question — is read to you as it is written, a sentence at a time, the first one as soon as it is finished: code blocks are left out, and code in a sentence is said as words (`cart.py:12` as "cart dot py line 12", `apply_discount` as "apply discount"). While it speaks, Ask's corner says so as it does while Clew listens — the speaker, how loud, and the button lit, which stops it — as do **Esc**, a new question and Ask's Stop; the voice key stops it and listens. With the microphone on you can also just talk over Clew, as you would over a person: the microphone stays open while Clew speaks, Clew's own voice taken out of what it hears by echo cancellation (WebRTC's AEC3, in Rust, through [sonora](https://github.com/dignifiedquire/sonora)). Clew pauses at your first words, stops once they are heard to be words — not its own voice heard back — and goes on where it paused if they were not. For the first few seconds it speaks after the microphone comes on, while the echo canceller learns the room, words said over it do not pause it: **Esc** and the voice key still do. Turn off **Talk over Clew while it speaks** in **Settings → Voice** (for loud speakers, say) and the microphone waits while Clew reads an answer aloud, opening again once Clew has finished. **Read answers aloud** in **Settings → Voice** says which answers are read: those to spoken questions (the default), every answer — typed questions too — or none. The voice is made on this computer too, by [ONNX Runtime](https://onnxruntime.ai) in `clew-speak`, a program beside Clew's own, and played on the system's default output — streamed: a sentence starts playing as soon as its first third of a second is made, and the rest plays as it is made. On a computer too slow to make speech as fast as it is heard, a sentence waits only until enough of it is made that it will not stop halfway.

**Voices.** Downloaded only when you click its **Download** in **Settings → Voice**, and accepted only if every file is, byte for byte, the one published at the revision Clew pins (checked by its SHA-256 and size): **Qwen3-TTS** ([Qwen](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-0.6B-CustomVoice), the 0.6B model with voices of its own, 719 MB, Apache-2.0, as [Quick Inference](https://huggingface.co/danyalmalik/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-0.6B-CustomVoice-ONNX-WebGPU) converts it to ONNX), which speaks English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian and Russian as a person does — in each language's own voice by default (Ryan for English, Vivian for Chinese, Ono Anna for Japanese, Sohee for Korean), or in any of its seven in every language; and **Supertonic 3** ([Supertone](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3), 145 MB, BigScience OpenRAIL-M), for twenty-two more — Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Arabic and Hindi among them — in ten voices, five female and five male. Each sentence is said in its own language: by its script for Chinese, Japanese and Korean, and by its words for English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch. **Read answers aloud**, the voice and the speed (0.8× to 1.5×) are set there too, and **Try** plays a sentence; they are kept in Clew's data folder (`voice/speech/`).

Voice works the same on macOS and Linux, as does everything else in Clew's window: the whole app is built and tested on both in CI, and each release ships it for both.

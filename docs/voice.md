# Voice

**English** · [简体中文](voice.zh-CN.md)

**Talking.** Press **⌘⇧S** (Ctrl+Shift+S on Linux and Windows; rebindable, and in the palette as **Talk**) or the microphone in Ask's message box, and say what you want. Clew listens for one thing said: the pause after it ends it, the microphone turns off, and what you said is acted on; with nothing said for eight seconds, it turns off too. To keep it listening, call it by name (see **Calling it by name** below): with **Start listening when you say “Hey, Clew”** on, the microphone, the voice key and "Hey, Clew" all start listening that goes on until you press either again or say "Bye, Clew" — each pause ends what you said, which is worked out in the background while it listens on for the next. With [Smart Turn](https://huggingface.co/pipecat-ai/smart-turn-v3) downloaded in **Settings → Voice** (9 MB), a pause is judged by how you said the last words: a finished sentence is acted on within half a second, and a pause to think, mid-sentence, is waited through for up to three seconds; it runs on this computer too, in `clew-speak`. What you said is then either a command or a question. A command is said the way you would say it, in any order, with or without "open" or "show"; one that costs something (an Explain All pass, starting or stopping the program, opening another folder) asks first, and you can answer aloud, "yes" or "no": listening for one thing said, the microphone stays on for that answer. A question goes to Ask and is asked at once. **Run spoken commands** and **Send spoken questions to Ask right away** can each be turned off in **Settings → Voice**. Voice shows what it is doing in Ask's corner, without opening the card: a pill there says it is listening, what a command did, or that a question is being answered, and is gone once there is nothing to say; its lit button stops listening, or Clew speaking. The answer is read aloud, and once Clew is no longer listening or speaking, a dot on **Ask**, on the tool strip, marks it until you open the card; an answer that will not be heard opens the card instead.

**Anything the window does.** Every command in the palette runs when you say its name in the language the window shows (all but **Talk**: the microphone is already on, and "Bye, Clew" turns it off), and so does each step of the debugger ("step over", "continue"), without a yes for each step. While a question waits on you, an answer it offers, said by its name, is that answer before any command of that name ("continue" to an agent asking "Continue?"): given if its button is in view; else held, and the agent's card shown, turned to its page, for you to say it again. A word that also closes things ("Cancel") first closes anything Esc would close before the card. "Debug continue" still steps the debugger. What needs a value is said with it, in English or the window's language:

**Hands-free.** Whatever the window shows can be pressed by saying it: a dialog's buttons, a setting's switch or choice, Ask's answers to the agent's questions, a list's rows — by the words on them, or by the name of what they belong to when several rows have the same button ("remove rust-analyzer"). Only the microphone's own button is not: say "Bye, Clew" instead. A dialog on top takes what you say as it takes the pointer, and a note or a condition being written takes your words as its text, until you say its **Save**. What removes or forgets something waits for a yes first, as a word may have been misheard; and a question whose Allow runs or trusts something takes your "yes" only once that Allow can be pressed, half a second after all of the question is in view; a "no" is taken at once. A "yes", or an answer that allows such as "Always allow", said sooner or while Clew's window is not in front, presses nothing and is not sent to Ask; and neither is any answer — a "no" too — said while Settings, a menu or the tour covers its question, or while Ask is put away: the status line asks you to say it again, and an Ask you put away comes back, on the page of the answer you named. While a question waits, what opens with a yes or a no — "yes, and run the tests too", "okay, why that file?", "don't run it" — answers nothing and is not sent to Ask either: the status line asks you to answer the question first, with a plain "yes" or "no". An answer that allows, said as a question ("Allow?"), answers nothing too: the status line asks for it said plainly. Where the question has no answer a plain "yes" or "no" gives — a card whose only way to decline is "Always reject" — or the word also closes what is on top ("cancel"), the status line asks for the words on the answer's button instead. An answer an agent names with a word that closes things ("Cancel") is given by saying that word with its card in view, once nothing Esc would close first is left open: a dialog, a menu or drop-down, a peek and, under a small card, Time Travel or the find bar; said while Clew speaks, the word only stops Clew. With **Run spoken commands** off, a "yes", a "no" or an answer's words said while a question waits are not sent to Ask either; answer it with its buttons. These are yours alone: the agent in Ask reaches the palette's commands and the settings, never a question's Allow.

**On your machine.** The microphone is read through the system's own audio (Core Audio, WASAPI, ALSA — which PipeWire and PulseAudio serve), the audio is resampled to 16 kHz, cut to where there is speech, and transcribed by whisper.cpp on this computer, on the GPU through Metal on a Mac and on the CPU elsewhere. Nothing is sent anywhere; only a question you choose to ask goes to your language model, as typed ones do.

**Calling it by name.** Turn on **Start listening when you say “Hey, Clew”** in **Settings → Voice**, and download the **Keyword Spotter** it needs there (33 MB: k2-fsa's streaming Zipformer for English and Mandarin, Apache-2.0, checked against its published size and SHA-256). Then, whenever the microphone is not listening, it waits — its button drawn in the accent color: say "Hey, Clew" and it listens, as if you had pressed the voice key, and "Hey, Clew, open the call graph" said in one breath works too. It hears the phrase said quietly, or from across the room — as quietly as Clew can still hear what you say after it. Something that only sounds like it ("Hey, cool, it works now") may wake the spotter, but what Clew then hears holds no "Clew": nothing of it is done, the status line says Clew did not hear its name, and it waits again — and if Clew was speaking, it only paused meanwhile, and goes on where it was. Clew's own voice saying the phrase, heard back, is no call either: what was heard with it is mostly Clew's own last words. Say "Bye, Clew" and it waits again — as it does after three minutes with nothing said, when it was called by name; the voice key and the microphone button still turn listening on and off. "Bye, Clew" ends listening at once: what you say right after it is not acted on. An answer being read goes on — **Esc** or the lit button stops it. "Hey, Clew" and "Bye, Clew" are said in English, whatever language the window is shown in; what you say after "Hey, Clew" can be in your own, and the call may then be written in it ("嘿，克鲁，打开设置") — it still counts. One window waits at a time: the one in front. While it waits, what the microphone hears goes only to the keyword spotter, which runs in `clew-speak` on this computer and knows that one phrase: nothing is kept or turned into words — only once the spotter hears the phrase are the last few seconds, the phrase among them, kept and worked out on this computer with what you say next, to hear whether it was a call.

**Models.** Whisper.cpp's own models, the same ones [Echo](https://github.com/damien-schneider/Echo) uses, downloaded only when you click a model's **Download** (its size is on the button) and accepted only if they are, byte for byte, the published file: **Small** (`small-q5_1`, 190 MB, the default), **Large Turbo** (`large-v3-turbo-q5_0`, 574 MB) and **Large** (`large-v3-q5_0`, 1081 MB), checked by their SHA-256 and exact size as Echo checks them, all of them multilingual; and Tiny and Base (English) for quick commands on slower machines, checked by the SHA-1 whisper.cpp lists. They are kept in Clew's data folder (`voice/`), and can be removed there. On macOS, the first time Clew listens, the system asks whether it may use the microphone.

**Answers, aloud.** The answer to a question you asked by voice — or, if you choose, to every question — is read to you as it is written, a sentence at a time, the first one as soon as it is finished: code blocks are left out, and code in a sentence is said as words (`cart.py:12` as "cart dot py line 12", `apply_discount` as "apply discount"). While it speaks, Ask's corner says so as it does while Clew listens — the speaker and how loud — and its lit button stops it, as do **Esc**, a new question and Ask's Stop; the voice key stops it and listens. With the microphone on you can also just talk over Clew, as you would over a person: the microphone stays open while Clew speaks, Clew's own voice taken out of what it hears by echo cancellation (WebRTC's AEC3, in Rust, through [sonora](https://github.com/dignifiedquire/sonora)). Clew pauses at your first words, stops once they are heard to be words — not its own voice heard back — and goes on where it paused if they were not. For the first few seconds it speaks after the microphone comes on, while the echo canceller learns the room, words said over it do not pause it: **Esc** and the voice key still do. Turn off **Talk over Clew while it speaks** in **Settings → Voice** (for loud speakers, say) and the microphone waits while Clew reads an answer aloud, opening again once Clew has finished; a "Hey, Clew" said while Clew's voice is heard is taken for its own, and not as a call. **Read answers aloud** in **Settings → Voice** says which answers are read: those to spoken questions (the default), every answer — typed questions too — or none. The voice is made on this computer too, by [ONNX Runtime](https://onnxruntime.ai) in `clew-speak`, a program beside Clew's own, and played on the system's default output — streamed: a sentence starts playing as soon as its first third of a second is made, and the rest plays as it is made. On a computer too slow to make speech as fast as it is heard, a sentence waits only until enough of it is made that it will not stop halfway.

**Voices.** Each is downloaded only when you click its **Download** — in **Settings → Voice**, or on the Ask card when an answer needs it — and accepted only if every file is, byte for byte, the one published at the revision Clew pins (checked by its SHA-256 and size): **Qwen3-TTS** ([Qwen](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-0.6B-CustomVoice), the 0.6B model with voices of its own, 719 MB, Apache-2.0, as [Quick Inference](https://huggingface.co/danyalmalik/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-0.6B-CustomVoice-ONNX-WebGPU) converts it to ONNX), which speaks English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian and Russian as a person does — in each language's own voice by default (Ryan for English, Vivian for Chinese, Ono Anna for Japanese, Sohee for Korean), or in any of its seven in every language; and **Supertonic 3** ([Supertone](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3), 145 MB, BigScience OpenRAIL-M), a smaller download for thirty-one languages — English, Korean, Japanese, Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Arabic and Hindi among them, but not Chinese — in ten voices, five female and five male. Each sentence is read by the best voice you have for its language: Qwen3-TTS where it is downloaded, else Supertonic 3. When none you have speaks it, the answer is shown, and the Ask card says which voice would read it, with a **Download** button and a **✕** that hides the notice until the window is closed. Once the voice is here, what Clew could not say is read — right after the sentence it is saying, if it is still reading the answer — as long as the answer is still the one on the card and you did not stop it. If the voice stops partway, the card says why, with a **Settings** button. Each sentence is said in its own language: by its script for Chinese, Japanese and Korean, and by its words for English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch. **Read answers aloud**, the voice and the speed (0.8× to 1.5×) are set in **Settings → Voice** too, and **Try** plays a sentence; the voices are kept in Clew's data folder (`voice/speech/`).

Voice works the same on macOS and Linux, as does everything else in Clew's window: the whole app is built and tested on both in CI, and each release ships it for both.
